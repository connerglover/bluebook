# AP Calculus BC — Unit Map

Distilled from the course packets so no PDF needs to be opened during generation. Textbook: Finney/Demana/Waits/Kennedy, *Calculus: Graphical, Numerical, Algebraic*, AP edition (ISBN 978-0-13-331161-7). Section numbers below match the packets.

Each unit lists what's taught, what the teacher emphasizes, and the mistakes the packets call out. Topic tags in **bold** are what the answer key should use.

---

## Unit 1 — Prerequisites for Calculus (1.1–1.6)

1.1 Lines · 1.2 Functions and Graphs · 1.3 Exponential Functions · 1.4 Parametric Equations · 1.5 Inverse Functions and Logarithms · 1.6 Trigonometric Functions

- **lines** — increments, slope, parallel and perpendicular conditions, point-slope and slope-intercept forms, building a linear model from data
- **functions** — domain and range, even/odd, symmetry, piecewise functions, absolute value, composition
- **exponential** — growth vs decay, the number *e*, fitting an exponential model
- **parametric** — modeling relations, circles, ellipses, and lines parametrically; eliminating the parameter
- **inverses-logs** — one-to-one functions, finding inverses, log properties, logarithmic models
- **trig** — radian measure, periodicity, even/odd trig functions, amplitude/period/phase shift, graphing all six functions, inverse trig

Emphasis: trig is flagged "ABSOLUTELY ESSENTIAL for the study of calculus." Parametric is called "goofy but doable."
Common errors: domain restrictions when composing; forgetting inverse trig range restrictions; sign errors in phase shift; assuming a parametric curve's orientation.
Typical stems: "Graph *y* = … and determine the period, amplitude, domain, range, and shifts." "Write an equation of the form *y* = *a* sin(*b*(*t* − *h*)) + *k* and superimpose it on a scatter plot."

## Unit 2 — Limits and Continuity (2.1–2.4)

2.1 Rates of Change and Limits · 2.2 Limits Involving Infinity · 2.3 Continuity · 2.4 Rates of Change, Tangent Lines, Sensitivity

- **avg-inst-rate** — average vs instantaneous rate of change
- **limits** — definition and properties, one- and two-sided limits, limits by substitution, Sandwich (Squeeze) Theorem
- **limits-infinity** — end behavior, horizontal and vertical asymptotes, end behavior models
- **continuity** — continuity at a point and on an interval, removable/jump/infinite discontinuities, extending a function continuously
- **ivt** — Intermediate Value Theorem
- **tangent-normal** — tangent lines as limits of secants, normal lines

Emphasis: tangent lines flagged "ABSOLUTELY CRITICAL." Limits called "tricky."
Common errors: assuming a two-sided limit exists when one-sided limits differ; confusing "limit exists" with "function is continuous"; dropping the absolute value in end-behavior work.
Typical stems: "Find all values of *a* that make *f* continuous at 2. Justify your answer." "Find a right end behavior model *g*(*x*) for *f*." "Use graphs and tables to find … then identify all horizontal asymptotes."

## Unit 3 — Derivatives (3.1–3.5)

3.1 Derivative of a Function · 3.2 Differentiability · 3.3 Rules for Differentiation · 3.4 Velocity and Other Rates of Change · 3.5 Derivatives of Trigonometric Functions

- **derivative-def** — limit definition and notation, derivative as a function
- **differentiability** — where derivatives fail to exist (corner, cusp, vertical tangent, discontinuity), differentiability implies continuity
- **rules** — power, constant multiple, sum, product, quotient; higher-order derivatives
- **motion** — position, velocity, speed, acceleration; when a body reverses direction; free-fall models
- **trig-derivatives** — derivatives of the six trig functions

Emphasis: the definition of the derivative is "ABSOLUTELY CRITICAL." Motion problems flagged "tricky."
Common errors: confusing velocity with speed; quotient rule numerator order; forgetting that differentiability implies continuity but not the converse.
Typical stems: "Find the lines that are tangent and normal to the curve … at *x* = …" "When does the body reverse direction?" "The equations for free fall near the surface of Mars and Jupiter are … how long would it take …"

## Unit 4 — More Derivatives (4.1–4.4)

4.1 Chain Rule · 4.2 Implicit Differentiation · 4.3 Derivatives of Inverse Trigonometric Functions · 4.4 Derivatives of Exponential and Logarithmic Functions

- **chain-rule** — composite functions, power chain rule, multi-step chains
- **parametric-slope** — slope of a parameterized curve, dy/dx from dy/dt ÷ dx/dt
- **implicit** — implicitly defined functions, dy/dx at a point, second derivatives implicitly
- **inverse-derivatives** — derivative of an inverse function at a point, inverse trig derivatives
- **exp-log-derivatives** — derivatives of *e*ˣ, *aˣ*, ln *x*, log_a *x*; logarithmic differentiation

Emphasis: the chain rule is flagged "THE MOST MISSED PROBLEM IN CALCULUS."
Common errors: dropping the inner derivative; mishandling *y*′ terms when differentiating implicitly; using the inverse-function derivative formula at the wrong point.
Typical stems: "Find *f*′(*x*) if …" "Find dy/dx at (2, 1) if …" "Find a derivative of the inverse of *y* = *x*² + 4 when *y* = 29."

## Unit 5 — Applications of Derivatives (5.1–5.6)

5.1 Extreme Values · 5.2 Mean Value Theorem · 5.3 Connecting f′ and f″ with the Graph of f · 5.4 Modeling and Optimization · 5.5 Linearization and Differentials · 5.6 Related Rates

- **extrema** — Extreme Value Theorem, absolute vs local extrema, critical vs stationary points, endpoint checks
- **mvt** — hypotheses and conclusion, existence-theorem reasoning, finding *c*
- **curve-analysis** — First and Second Derivative Tests, increasing/decreasing, concavity, points of inflection
- **optimization** — mathematical, business, and economic models
- **linearization** — linear approximation, differentials, absolute and relative change
- **related-rates** — implicit differentiation with respect to time

Emphasis: the packet shouts "DON'T FORGET TO CHECK ENDPOINTS" and "WATCH OUT FOR ENDPOINTS."
Common errors: skipping endpoint evaluation on a closed interval; concluding an extremum from f′(c) = 0 without a sign change; confusing conclusions drawn from f′ with those from f″; forgetting to verify MVT hypotheses before applying it.
Typical stems: "Identify each *x*-value at which any absolute extreme value occurs. Explain how your answer is consistent with the Extreme Value Theorem." "Show that *f* satisfies the hypothesis of the MVT on [0,2] and find a solution *c*." "Use the First Derivative Test to find the local extreme values. Identify any absolute extrema."

## Unit 6 — The Definite Integral (6.1–6.5) — **NO CALCULATOR on the test**

6.1 Estimating with Finite Sums · 6.2 Definite Integrals · 6.3 Definite Integrals and Antiderivatives · 6.4 Fundamental Theorem of Calculus · 6.5 Trapezoidal Rule

- **finite-sums** — LRAM, MRAM, RRAM; distance from a velocity function
- **riemann** — Riemann sums, sigma notation, the definite integral as a limit
- **definite-integral** — integral as area, properties, average value of a function
- **ftc** — Part 1 (derivative of an accumulation function) and Part 2 (evaluation)
- **trapezoid** — trapezoidal rule, T = (LRAM + RRAM)/2, over- vs underestimate from concavity

Emphasis: "what a definite integral is and how it relates to area" is flagged CRITICALLY IMPORTANT.
Common errors: wrong Δx; misreading which endpoint a rule uses; predicting over/underestimate without checking concavity; forgetting the chain rule in d/dx ∫₀^{x²}.
Typical stems: "Use the Trapezoidal Rule with *n* = 4 to approximate … Use the concavity of the function to predict whether the approximation is an overestimate or an underestimate." "Use the function values in the table and the Trapezoidal Rule with *n* = 6 to approximate ∫₂⁸ f(x) dx." "On what interval or intervals is *F* increasing? Justify your answer."

## Unit 7 — Differential Equations and Mathematical Modeling (7.1–7.5)

7.1 Slope Fields and Euler's Method · 7.2 Antidifferentiation by Substitution · 7.3 Antidifferentiation by Parts · 7.4 Exponential Growth and Decay · 7.5 Logistic Growth

- **slope-fields** — constructing and reading slope fields
- **eulers** — Euler's Method (flagged "easy, but nobody can do it")
- **substitution** — u-substitution, including definite integrals with changed limits
- **by-parts** — integration by parts, trig and log integrands
- **separable** — separable differential equations, initial value problems
- **growth-decay** — exponential models, Newton's Law of Cooling
- **logistic** — logistic growth, carrying capacity
- **partial-fractions** — partial fractions in integration ("time-consuming but doable")

Emphasis: the 7.1–7.3 quiz is **no calculator**. Antidifferentiation is flagged "extremely difficult."
Common errors: forgetting +C; not changing limits on a substituted definite integral; picking the wrong *u* and *dv*; dropping the constant when solving an IVP.
Typical stems: "Find the general solution to the exact differential equation." "Solve the initial value problem explicitly." "Construct a slope field for the differential equation. Use *x*-values from −1 to 1 and *y*-values from −1 to 2."

## Unit 8 — Applications of Definite Integrals (8.1–8.5) — final exam only

8.1 Integral as Net Change · 8.2 Areas in the Plane · 8.3 Volumes · 8.4 Lengths of Curves · 8.5 Applications from Science and Statistics

- **net-change** — the integral as an accumulator for any rate
- **area-between** — area between two curves, integrating with respect to *y*
- **volumes** — known cross sections (squares, semicircles, triangles), disks and washers, revolving about an axis
- **arc-length** — L = ∫√(1 + (dy/dx)²) dx, curves with a cusp, vertical tangent, or corner
- **science-stats** — work, fluid force, probability density applications

Emphasis: the packet labels volumes "pure evil" and opens the chapter with "Abandon all hope, ye who enter here." Cross-section volumes are the hard part.
Common errors: integrating with respect to the wrong variable; forgetting to square the radius; using the diameter as the radius; wrong limits when curves cross.
Typical stems: "The solid lies between planes perpendicular to the *x*-axis at *x* = 0 and *x* = 4. The cross sections … are squares whose diagonals run from *y* = … to *y* = … Find the volume of the solid analytically." "Find the exact length of the curve …"
